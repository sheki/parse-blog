# Name it to tame it

- Use precise "I feel" statements: Always follow "I feel" with an actual emotion word (e.g., "I feel worried"). Never follow it with a thought or judgment like "I feel like everyone is out to get me".
- Separate emotion from identity: Say "I feel sad" instead of "I am depressed." This keeps you in the role of an observer rather than letting the emotion define your identity, giving you the power to change it.
- Be hyper-specific: Do not settle for broad terms like "angry" if you are actually "irritated," "furious," or "bitter." Pinpointing the exact word gives you more power to resolve the feeling.
- Track your emotions daily: Set a recurring daily alarm. When it goes off, physically write down the exact emotions you are experiencing in that exact moment.

# A non judgemental attitude

- Stop labeling emotions as good or bad: Instead, classify them as "comfortable" or "uncomfortable." This subtle shift stops the instinct to fight the emotion and opens up mental space to respond to it.
- Describe rather than judge: Act like a curious scientist. When you feel a difficult emotion, describe exactly what it feels like and where it sits in your body instead of telling yourself you shouldn't be feeling it [10:13].
- Acknowledge to process: Uncomfortable emotions like worry or disappointment serve a function. Acknowledge what they are trying to tell you, sit with the discomfort, and make a conscious choice about how to act, rather than trying to distract yourself

# Function of negative emotions

- Treat emotions as warning signs: Do not suppress uncomfortable feelings. Instead, pause and ask yourself what specific problem the emotion is trying to alert you to.
- Take direct action to resolve the root cause: If guilt tells you that you did something wrong, apologize or make repairs. If fear warns of danger, take steps to create safety. Acting on the warning naturally dissipates the emotion.
- Stop relying on coping skills: Never use distraction or temporary coping mechanisms as a permanent solution. Coping only buries the emotion temporarily; you must resolve the actual issue to create sustainable emotional peace.
